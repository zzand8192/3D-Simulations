## Suggested sound layers

| Layer | Story purpose | Example planning choice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ambience | Establishes the place | Restaurant room tone, city, wind, water |
| Character action | Makes an ordinary task physical | Paper movement, cup placement, footsteps |
| Dialogue | Communicates a response or information | Brief exchange with a cashier |
| Event onset | Marks the rule changing | A motivated transient or sudden texture change |
| Approach | Makes distance and anticipation audible | Increasing intensity or changed tonal content |
| Contact | Confirms the visual arrival | One dominant synchronized event |
| Perspective | Reinforces camera location | Enclosed, distant, submerged, or obstructed sound |
| Music | Supports mood and pace | A restrained bed that leaves room for action |

These are production recommendations and should be chosen per episode.

## Narration and dialogue

Use the inherited narrator-free production default unless Isaac revises it. Convey information through action, UI, natural dialogue, and occasional news coverage. Greg speaks briefly when a line adds something his gesture cannot. A news presenter can speak within the episode's world, with the broadcast clearly grounded in a screen or source.

## Plan the mix with the story

Identify the sound the viewer is waiting for. Reduce competing sounds around an important quiet reaction or delayed arrival. Preserve audible speech. Change the sound perspective when the camera changes position. Check the export on headphones and a phone speaker. Record actual peak and loudness checks using the editor's meters, with final targets selected for the platform.

## Edit for information

A shot remains useful while it reveals or changes something. Cut when the next shot supplies a new fact, scale, reaction, or consequence. Plan text hold time alongside action. Mark slow motion and time jumps. Use a brief pause before a consequence when the audience understands the impending result.

## Ending and sound tail

Let the main sound resolve with the last meaningful image. Keep a short closing card only as long as its information needs. The source examples often retain sound through the card. Choose the tail deliberately and check that the file ends cleanly.
