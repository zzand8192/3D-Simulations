# Studio brief

## Purpose

Create cinematic 3D What If simulations that make an impossible change understandable, visually satisfying, and worth following to its final consequence. The audience should be able to identify the altered rule, observe its effects, and anticipate a meaningful endpoint.

## Confirmed creative direction

Isaac requested cinematic Roblox-inspired 3D What If videos, a recurring supporting character named Greg, and comprehensive analysis of successful-looking reference examples. Greg wears a baseball cap, a blue hoodie, and black pants. He is often unlucky. After the simulation has developed, a cutaway can show him reading a newspaper, ordering food, or performing another ordinary task that the event interrupts.

Isaac clarified that Greg can be in a different place in each separate simulation, almost as though he keeps ending up lost. An earlier interpretation placed him in multiple countries during one episode. The later clarification supersedes that interpretation.

Isaac explicitly compared Greg's intended recurring function to the recurring tsunami payoff in the references. Viewers should learn to expect his presence while remaining curious about his location and circumstances.

## Working production defaults

The retrieved prior chat contains assistant-proposed defaults of at least 60 seconds per simulation, no fixed maximum, no external narrator, natural dialogue, optional fictional news coverage, environmental sound, subtle music, and an elegant white serif opening question that transitions toward smaller informational labels. Preserve these as the starting production brief, with their status as inherited defaults. They were not all independently confirmed by Isaac in the available user messages.

The reviewed references support vertical composition and 30 fps as useful starting formats. Recommend 1080 x 1920 delivery at 30 fps for new production, subject to the actual platform and renderer. The source references themselves are 576 x 1024.

## Audience promise

Each video gives the audience a clear hypothetical change, surprising consequences, a measurable progression, a physical sense of scale, and a particular final image. Greg adds recognition and situational humor across episodes.

## Creative pillars

1. Understandable cause and effect. Show what changes before asking the audience to track several consequences.
2. Visible progression. Numbers and pictures should move toward a recognizable endpoint.
3. Ordinary details. A meal, a newspaper, a phone, a cup, or a bus stop can make a worldwide event personal.
4. Specific spectacle. Choose a visual that follows from the premise and changes as the scene progresses.
5. Recognition with variation. Preserve Greg's identity and vary his place, task, entrance, and outcome.
6. Consistent presentation. Reuse UI hierarchy, text treatment, sound logic, and production standards.

## What the studio should learn from the references

Adopt the storytelling mechanisms and build original assets, scenes, and outcomes. The references are useful for studying timing, readable scale, UI, anticipation, and callbacks. Their downloaded watermarks identify the local footage but do not establish original authorship or ownership. Source attribution fields are provided for later completion.

## Open decisions

- Finished Greg face, body, cap shape, exact palette, voice, and rig.
- Final channel display name, logo, platform mix, and release schedule.
- Preferred renderer, animation workflow, and editor.
- Whether each project keeps the inherited narrator-free default.
- Final UI font family and project-specific export settings.

No revenue target, audience demographic, production team, upload commitment, or completed asset is assumed.
