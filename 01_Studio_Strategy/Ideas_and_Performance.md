# Ideas and performance

## Develop an idea

Write the altered rule in a sentence. Define when it starts, where it applies, how long it lasts, and what still follows ordinary physics. List the assumptions needed to make the experiment coherent. Then choose the most useful examples to show on screen.

Score each criterion from 1 to 5 as an internal planning aid. Scores are judgments and do not predict view counts.

| Criterion | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Immediate comprehension | Can someone understand the opening without background explanation? |
| Visual change | Can three moments look substantially different? |
| Escalation | Does the progression reveal new consequences? |
| Human detail | Can an ordinary task make the effect personal? |
| Endpoint | Is there a particular image or event worth waiting for? |
| Greg opportunity | Can Greg demonstrate a consequence naturally? |
| Production fit | Can the current assets and schedule deliver it clearly? |

Select ideas that can be demonstrated. A premise with a dramatic title still needs a readable sequence of consequences.

## Build a hook

Use a familiar subject plus a precise alteration. Examples of structures include 'What if [material] disappeared for [duration]?' and 'What if [ordinary object] became [multiplier] larger?' These are planning patterns, not a required title formula.

Sketch three opening frames. Test whether the question, main subject, and first change are understandable at phone size. Write what the viewer should expect to see later. Ensure the final sequence delivers that expectation or a clearly connected development.

## Study real audience response

After publishing, record the platform, date, duration, impressions or reach when available, views, average watch time, completion rate when provided, shares, saves, comments, follows, and retention-curve observations. Keep platform definitions attached to the numbers. A replay can push average watch time beyond runtime on some services.

Derived watch-time ratio equals average watch time divided by runtime. It is an internal comparison and should be labeled separately from a platform-reported completion rate. Views alone do not explain whether the hook, audience distribution, topic, or ending drove performance.

## Review the curve

If viewers leave early, inspect the opening's clarity and delivery of the promised subject. If a decline starts during explanation, inspect repeated information and readability. If attention rises near a return to Greg, compare the surrounding shots before attributing the change to him. If a closing card loses viewers after the payoff, consider shortening it in a future upload.

## Run a controlled creative experiment

Change one major choice at a time where practical. Compare a visible opening consequence with a slower setup, or one clear UI measurement with several simultaneous figures. Record the premise, runtime, distribution context, and outcome. Treat small or mismatched samples as tentative.

The business should accumulate its own evidence. The reference pack provides hypotheses to test, and the upload log records what actually happens.
