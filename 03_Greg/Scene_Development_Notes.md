## Activity bank

Use activities as starting points and choose the one that fits the premise. Reading a newspaper gives a quiet attention shift. Ordering food gives a short exchange and a waiting object. Paying for something gives a small goal and a possible interrupted transaction. Waiting for a bus gives an expected arrival. Carrying a drink makes motion visible. Shopping gives shelves and a selection. Watching television can connect to an in-world news report. Resting on a park bench gives a stable composition for environmental change.

## Variation bank

Vary his location, degree of awareness, initial mood, task, nearby character, object, and response. A scene can end in inconvenience, partial success, a practical adaptation, or an unresolved setup. A background sighting can later become a featured scene.

## Meaningful recurring details

Once the model is approved, viewers should recognize its silhouette and clothing. A pause or a glance can become a familiar acting tendency, with enough variation to remain responsive to the scene. A newspaper or drink can return as a prop across episodes when it fits, though neither has been confirmed as mandatory.

## Prepare an AI request

Ask for three different Greg scenes for the same premise. Require a location, task, consequence, reaction, production duration, and explanation of what each adds. Compare them for clarity and production effort. Select one before requesting a full shot plan.
