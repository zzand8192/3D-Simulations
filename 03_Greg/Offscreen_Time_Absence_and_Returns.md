# Offscreen time, absence, and returns

Greg's recurring presence should create recognition across simulations. The main simulation still needs room to establish and develop. This document covers what happens before he appears, between appearances, and when considering a special episode without a featured Greg scene.

## Before he appears

Show the hypothetical rule and its first consequences. A returning viewer may already be looking for him. Place his featured scene when it adds a new personal-scale consequence. A background sighting can reward attention, though its inclusion remains a flexible choice.

## While he is offscreen

Keep the state of his last scene recorded. If he was holding a newspaper, waiting for an order, or moving toward a destination, a later return should follow that situation. The audience does not need a continuous account of every minute, but the production should know what changed.

## A later return

Return for a new result. A task that looked solvable may now be impossible, a possession may have changed, or Greg may have found a practical adaptation. Use the same prop or goal to make the connection readable. Change the image enough that the return contributes a payoff.

## Considering a full absence

Isaac has asked for Greg to be recurring. A policy for how many episodes may omit him has not been decided. Treat a proposal to leave him out of a whole episode as a creative choice to state explicitly, with a reason based on the episode. Avoid inventing a mandatory absence schedule or presenting an omission as an approved series rule.

## Tracking

Use the appearance log to record his featured scenes, background sightings, returns, and proposed absences. Review it when planning the next episode so his recurrence stays intentional.
