## Premise

Altered rule: at one moment, every constructed road on Earth disappears, including highways, streets, rural roads, road bridges, road surfaces, markings and crosswalks.

Onset, scope, duration and restoration: the change is instant and worldwide (DAY #1, 0:05.35 on screen). It is permanent. The original roads never return, although new roads can be built (Month 6 onward).

Fictional assumptions:
- The natural ground under the roads remains about 0.3 m lower and is visible as dirt.
- Sidewalks, buildings, railways, pedestrian paths, traffic lights and signs remain.
- Vehicles remain. Moving cars drop onto the ground and skid to a stop.
- Elevated road failures appear only in distant, non-graphic shots with emergency response.

Factual claims and sources: there are no numeric claims. Cities and countries are named only as broadcast locations.

## Audience promise

Opening image: a busy New York avenue with the centered question.

What the viewer waits to see: the roads vanish (a close-up of a tyre, then the wide reveal), then how the world copes.

Three distinct consequences:
1. Transport stops dead, from cities to highways.
2. Supply chains break by Day 3, so groceries, hospitals, rail and airports are affected.
3. Transport is redesigned by Month 6, with rail, bikes, utility carts and new roads.

Final meaningful image: Year 5, a rebuilt city with new roads, elevated rail, bike lanes and green roofs, seen as the camera rises over the skyline.
